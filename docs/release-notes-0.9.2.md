# cuttr 0.9.2

A way back out of a take, the lane colours where there is room for them, and no
tracking marks over people's faces while you are watching the film.

## Out of a take, and out of a scene

A back chevron at the head of the bar, between the traffic lights and the
document's name, which is where one lives in everything else on this machine —
and the same direction the capsule reads in, so "out of this and up to that" is
left to right.

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  ●  ●  ●   ‹  Lilly, A…, Chris 1 │ main  ⇧⌘P │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

A scene always has one, because a scene is opened *from* a project. A take has
one when an open project actually lists it — not merely when some project is
open, because a chevron that took you to a programme your take is not in would
be worse than no chevron. A project never has one; it is the outermost thing
there is.

## The lane colours moved

The bar was filling up from the left and leaving half of itself empty: the name,
a rule, the monitor, six lane colours and a `…` all crowded against the traffic
lights, with everything past the clock standing in air.

Which lane the next cut lands on is reached for rather than read, so it is the
half that can move. It now sits on the far side of the clock.

## Anchors are off while you watch

Tracking marks are for *making* the film. A tracked face is a thing you place an
overlay against, and once it is placed the dots are in the way of seeing the
thing they placed. The preview page and full screen are both "show me the film",
and a film with marks over everybody's faces is not the film.

They are off by default in both, and not off for good — the switch is still on
the picture. Whatever you had chosen on the editing side is what comes back when
you return to it.

## Requirements

macOS 14 or newer, Apple silicon or Intel. Transcription, sound detection and
proposed names need macOS 26; proposed names also need Apple Intelligence
switched on. Everything they do happens on this Mac.
